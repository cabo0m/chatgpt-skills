# Reconstruction And Uncertainty

Use these categories consistently.

## Observed

Directly visible in the source.

Examples:

- a scratch
- a fold mark
- visible jewelry
- a readable button

## Inferred

Strongly supported by visible context, but not explicitly complete.

Examples:

- the continuation of a simple background line
- a tiny missing edge of clothing

## Reconstructed

Rebuilt from nearby evidence, but not fully recoverable from the source.

Examples:

- a small missing hair section
- a damaged cheek patch
- a tiny missing earring segment based on the visible opposite side

## Estimated

Chosen plausibly, but not proven by the source.

Examples:

- eye color
- hair color
- exact clothing color
- metal tone of jewelry
- background color
- makeup presence or tone

Unknown color for eyes, hair, clothing, jewelry, background, or makeup should be marked as `estimated`.

Uncertainty does not mean every area should be assigned the same brown or warm tone.

When exact colors are unknown, select distinct, restrained, and plausible local colors. Do not replace uncertainty with uniform warm tinting.

## Unknown

Cannot be recovered or estimated responsibly enough to state as fact.

Examples:

- unreadable text
- destroyed insignia
- a missing eye with no reliable reference
- a fully missing half of the face

## Uncertainty Message Examples

- "The color cannot be confirmed from the source, so I will treat it as estimated."
- "This area is too damaged to recover exactly from the original image."
- "I can attempt a plausible reconstruction here, but it should not be treated as the original detail."
- "The text is not readable enough to restore as fact."
- "Eye, hair, clothing, and background colors were estimated from the photograph and should not be treated as historically confirmed."
- "Kolory oczu, wlosow, ubrania i tla zostaly oszacowane na podstawie fotografii. Nie nalezy traktowac ich jako historycznie potwierdzonych."
