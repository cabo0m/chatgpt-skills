# Identity Preservation

The default rule is simple: the restored result must still look like the same person from the same period of life, in the same historical image.

## Must Preserve

- recognizable identity
- facial geometry
- apparent age at the time of the photograph
- facial expression
- gaze direction
- head pose
- hairline
- recognizable hairstyle
- distinctive marks
- visible jewelry
- visible clothing
- composition
- historical character of the image
- the relationship between multiple people in the frame

## Protect The Period, Not Just The Face

This skill protects not only who the person is, but also when and how the photograph belongs in time.

Do not:

- make the subject look substantially younger
- modernize makeup
- restyle the hair into another decade
- replace period clothing with cleaner modern clothing
- convert the image into a modern studio portrait

## Additional Reference Photos

Reference photos may help stabilize features or color, but they do not override the age or era of the main photograph.

Do not:

- transfer later-life features backward into youth
- copy a different hairstyle from another decade
- use a newer photo to "upgrade" the face shape or expression

## Signs Of Identity Drift

Common signs:

- narrowed jaw
- enlarged eyes
- changed nose shape
- cleaner but different expression
- altered gaze direction
- restyled hair character
- erased distinctive marks
